# Terminal companion

The white scout is the terminal's animated player sidekick. His larger, straight-on head addresses the player by default. Opening the terminal reveals his head and upper chest; the lower chest and relaxed arms stay behind its border. Greetings, completed records, celebrations and stretches raise his solid mitten hands into view. Glances, scans and travel occasionally turn his head sideways. Closing the terminal with a selected target exposes approximately half his sleeping head. Clearing the target hides him. The chart, Log, Settings and main menu hide him to keep their interfaces clear.

His expressions react locally to input, scanning, completed records, clearing the screen, travel, warp, arrivals, first landings, samples and warnings. Quiet moments include breathing, blinking, glancing and stretching. Closed-terminal reactions remain asleep. This adds body language without a remote AI service or changes to commands.

The renderer has a native **64 × 72 pixel** canvas. The head is 52 × 44, chest 26 × 24 and each arm 10 × 20. The atlas is downsampled once with nearest-neighbor sampling, binary alpha and a fourteen-color palette. Expressions use that palette; all final drawing coordinates are integers. Head turns stretch the already-rendered face with nearest-neighbor sampling to avoid soft fractional expression edges. Painting is capped at 20 frames per second. At rest only the top 52 rows of the awake sprite are visible; the stage clips the remaining body behind the terminal. Sleeping clips to roughly 20 rows with the eyes still visible inside the visor.

The avatar follows the visible terminal pocket's top edge during its slide. It shrinks with limited space above an enlarged terminal and avoids camera rectangles and nearby controls without narrowing the game's viewport. It never intercepts input. Arm raises and turns ease toward their poses. Reduced motion stops bobbing, turning, arm raises, pulses, sparkles and the reveal transition while preserving expressions.

## Artwork

`assets/terminal-companion.png` is the unmodified transparent **1254 × 1254** output from the built-in image-generation tool in edit mode, revising the previous atlas to the user's simpler, front-facing white scout design. It has separate head, chest and arm components; the new hands are solid paddles with no fingers. Measured alpha bounds live in `src/ui/terminal-companion.js`. Code draws eyes and mouths on the blank visor. The PNG is included in the offline shell.

Exact generation prompt:

> Use case: style-transfer. Edit target: attached robot component atlas. Replace its highly detailed artwork with VERY SIMPLE chunky 16-bit console pixel sprites. Keep the same white scout robot identity, white armor, teal accents, cyan star emblem, side antenna, forehead crest, blank dark visor, separate head/chest/two arms and transparent background. Make the HEAD perfectly straight front-facing toward the viewer: symmetrical left and right ears, horizontal visor and cheek armor, even size on both sides, no three-quarter angle, no tilt or perspective. The crest should be short and centered. Simplify drastically to a low-resolution 52 by 40 pixel head, a 26 by 24 pixel chest, and 10 by 20 pixel arms, all shown enlarged with exact square pixels. Use 12 flat colors total, thick navy outlines, two-tone shading only, large solid blocks. No tiny mechanical details, screws, scratches, textures, dithering, gradients, shiny 3D modeling or soft edges. ARM ENDS MUST BE SOLID ROUNDED MITTEN PADDLES: no fingers, no thumbs, no articulated digits, no claws or pincers. White forearm shell, simple teal hinge, one-piece smooth mitten end. Render a square transparent sprite sheet with HEAD alone in upper-left quadrant, CHEST alone in upper-right quadrant, left arm alone lower-left and right arm alone lower-right. Components clearly separated with generous transparent spacing, all straight front-view orthographic and perfectly consistent, centered within cells, visor blank with NO eyes or mouth so game code animates them. The overall feel should be a friendly simple SNES-era character, iconic at a glance rather than a detailed illustration. This is a game production atlas, no captions, no backdrop, no dividers, no floor shadow. Actual alpha transparency.
